Privacy policy

Finanças · last updated October 12, 2026

What the app does with your data

Finanças runs only on your own computer. The bank statements you import and everything the app derives from them are stored on that computer, encrypted with a key kept in your operating system's password store. The app has no servers and no accounts, and it collects no analytics, telemetry or personal information. Nobody but you has access to your data.

Google Drive

If you choose to connect Google Drive, the app uploads its backup files to a folder in your own Drive. Each backup is encrypted on your computer with a password only you know before it is uploaded, so neither Google nor anyone else can read it.

  • The app requests only the drive.file permission: it can see and manage only the files and folders it created itself, plus a folder you explicitly choose for it in Google's own folder picker. It never sees anything else in your Drive.
  • It uses this access only to create or use its backup folder, upload backups, and delete its own older backups (keeping the newest ten).
  • The access token is stored in your operating system's password store on your computer and is never sent anywhere other than Google.
  • You can disconnect at any time in the app's settings, or revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

The app's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other network access

The app downloads public market data (exchange rates, asset prices and interest rate indexes). These requests contain no personal information.

If you choose to watch a bitcoin wallet by its public key, the app looks up the wallet's addresses (never the key itself) on a blockchain server to read their transactions. By default that is mempool.space, which can see the addresses looked up together and your IP address; you can point the app to your own node instead.
